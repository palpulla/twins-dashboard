# Reviewer Prompt

Copy everything below the line and send it with
`2026-08-09-meta-overhaul-reviewer-handoff.md` attached (or pasted underneath).

Works for a human media buyer or an AI model. If sending to a human, delete the
"Output format" section and just ask for their notes.

---

I run Twins Garage Doors, a family-run residential garage door company in Madison,
Wisconsin. I just ended a $2,400/month agency relationship and am taking Meta ads
in-house. Attached is a proposed teardown and rebuild of my Meta ad account, written
against a live audit of the account and my own job and revenue data.

I want you to review it critically before I execute any of it. I am not looking for
encouragement. I am looking for the things that are wrong, missing, or naive.

**Your role:** an experienced paid-social buyer who has run home-services accounts at
small local budgets, and who has seen plenty of plans like this fail. Assume the author
is competent but may be over-confident and may have talked themselves into a structure
that sounds tidy but will not survive contact with a $90/day budget.

**What is fixed and not worth arguing about.** Please do not spend your review on these,
they are my decisions and they are final:

- Budget is $90/day, roughly $2,700/month. I cannot raise it right now.
- Geography is Madison WI plus 25 miles. Milwaukee is deferred.
- I want all four offers tested: urgent repair, $49 tune-up, install with financing, and
  free estimate / $0 service call.
- I appear on camera personally. The AI-generated spokesperson is retired and is not
  coming back.

**What I actually want from you:**

1. **Attack the structure.** The plan groups four offers into two "conversion action"
   cells at $55 and $35 per day, rather than four separate campaigns, because four cells
   would sit below Meta's learning threshold. Is that the right response to the
   constraint? Section 4 of the attachment lists two alternatives that were considered
   and rejected. If you think one of the rejected options is actually correct, say so and
   say why.

2. **Answer the seven questions in Section 9.** Those are the calls the author flagged as
   least confident. Give a direct position on each, not a survey of considerations.

3. **Check the arithmetic and the reasoning that rests on it.** Especially the
   learning-phase math in Section 4 and the cost thresholds in Section 6. Those
   thresholds derive from owner-estimated close rates, not measured ones, and I want to
   know if that makes them unusable.

4. **Find blindspots not already listed.** Section 10 names eight the author already
   knows about. Tell me what is not on that list. This is the highest-value part of your
   review.

5. **Tell me what would make this fail.** Not generic risk, specifically: what is the most
   likely way this plan is a waste of the next 90 days and $8,000?

6. **Say what you would do differently at this exact budget**, if your answer is
   materially different from the plan. Be concrete enough that I could execute it.

**Ground rules:**

- Where the attachment tags something [VERIFIED], treat it as real data. Where it tags
  something [ASSUMED] or [UNKNOWN], those are the soft spots. Go there first.
- If you think a piece of evidence is being over-read, say so. One example the author may
  be over-reading: a carousel ad that hit 3.07% CTR with zero leads is being treated as
  "good creative, wrong objective." The pessimistic reading is that it just harvested
  curiosity clicks and the concept will not convert. I want a real opinion on which it is.
- Do not soften your assessment to be agreeable. If the plan is basically sound and only
  needs three tweaks, say that plainly too. I would rather hear "this is fine, stop
  overthinking it" than receive invented criticism.
- If you need information that is not in the attachment, list what you need rather than
  assuming a value.

**Output format:**

1. **Verdict** in two sentences: proceed, proceed with changes, or rework.
2. **Top 3 changes** you would make, in priority order, each with the reasoning.
3. **Answers to the seven questions** in Section 9, numbered to match.
4. **Blindspots not already listed** in Section 10.
5. **Most likely failure mode** for this plan.
6. **What you would do differently**, if materially different.
7. **What you need from me** that was not provided.

Be as detailed as you need to be. Length is not a problem. Vagueness is.
